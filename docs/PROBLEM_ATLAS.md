# Problem Atlas: definition, scope, research method

> This document operationalizes the database principles; it is not a second authority. For the current normative record, query `atlas.current_principles` or consult the versioned mirror linked in [PRODUCT_VISION.md](PRODUCT_VISION.md).

## What is a problem space?

A **bounded, revisable grouping of related human, operational or systemic frictions that obstruct valued outcomes**, described independently of whether products currently exist. Explain **who** encounters it, **when/where** it happens, **why it persists**, **how people adapt**, **what institutions offer**, **what evidence exists**, and **how it connects** to other problems.

A diagnosis, demographic, industry vertical, product category, collection of similar companies or TAM estimate is **not by itself** a problem space. Multiple memberships and overlaps are legitimate.

### Four nested objects, with explicit crosslinks

- **Landscape:** broad map of ageing and care, its forces, tensions and actors.
- **Problem space:** coherent explanatory territory with scope and boundaries, not a folder of companies.
- **Problem:** concrete, falsifiable description of a recurring obstacle or failure in a specific situation and population.
- **Scene / evidence / response:** documented accounts, daily workflows, workarounds, causes, policies, clinical/provider services and products illuminating the problem.

The zoom structure is editorial; don't treat map coordinates or territory area as measurements.

## Two independent research pulls

**Bottom-up: lived experience**
- People's aspirations, identities, relationships, desired jobs and roles, daily practices and compromises.
- What they actually do: creative adaptations, analog alternatives, DIY fixes, informal coordination, intentional avoidance, non-adoption.
- Whose voice is represented; age, context, geography, setting, accessibility; whose voice is absent.
- Never infer a universal aspiration from a single quote or scenario.

**Top-down: organized responses**
- Health, long-term care, housing, social services, institutional workflows, insurance/reimbursement, public infrastructure, labor arrangements, regulation and care-provider offerings.
- Who controls budgets, who bears effort/cost, who benefits, who measures success, and whose incentives conflict.
- A service offering demonstrates a response, **not** proof of prevalence, effectiveness, or remaining unmet need.

**Mismatch / synthesis**
- Where lived aspirations and available services clash, where people create workarounds, where transitions fail, where incentives externalize costs.
- Label causality as **hypothesis** until supported.
- Preserve tensions, conflicting accounts and cases with no obvious commercial payer.

## Multi-stakeholder graph

Study older adults, care recipients of different ages where relevant, professional carers, family/friend carers, clinicians, provider operators, payers, communities and entrepreneurs. A problem can affect many of these; distinguish direct experience, indirect consequence and decision power. Stakeholder perspectives are **lenses on one graph**, never competing problem taxonomies.

## Definition of done for a problem-space record

A reviewable record contains:
1. Plain-language name and 2–3-sentence explanation of *why it matters*.
2. Inclusion/exclusion boundaries, geography and care settings.
3. Related specific problem statements and connections to other spaces.
4. Affected stakeholders and valued outcomes; explicit competing interests.
5. Real-life situations and **observed** workarounds where documented; hypothetical ones clearly labelled.
6. Proposed root causes, mechanisms and institutional/economic constraints with epistemic status.
7. Existing responses including noncommercial, public and informal interventions; company links only after independent problem definition.
8. Attributed sources, source scopes, counterevidence, and research gaps.
9. Question(s) for further investigation, not an AI-created opportunity verdict.

## Taxonomy discipline

The prior **16 top-level domains** and subsequent **48 domains / 384 problem candidates / 50 sources** are historical research starting points, **not validated completeness**. Import only accessible originals, preserve provenance, and never automatically promote a candidate to a documented problem. Do not assign legacy `Txx` company tags as the atlas taxonomy.

Select deep dives for their ability to clarify a key mechanism, illustrate diverse experiences and connect across spaces, not for company counts or assumed market potential. Coverage must explicitly show missing knowledge.

## Example without preselecting companies

**Space: Sustainable professional care work.** Valued outcomes: caregivers can do meaningful, safe, fairly compensated work; recipients receive respectful consistent care; providers can sustain delivery. Problems might involve documentation burden, shift unpredictability, handoff failures, physical injury and inadequate development opportunities. Investigate worker interviews/diaries **and** scheduling, provider economics, reimbursement, regulatory obligations. Only afterwards connect existing services and products. All statements need scoped evidence before publication.
