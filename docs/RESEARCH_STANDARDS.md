# Who Cares research and publishing standards

> Supporting methodology. Normative principle authority remains in `atlas.current_principles`. See [PROBLEM_ATLAS.md](PROBLEM_ATLAS.md) for the research object definition.

## Objective

Create a broad, source-linked, revisable atlas of **problems**, not a flattering collection of product opportunities. Discover them independently of companies by comparing lived experience with organizational and economic responses.

## Source sweeps

Research across disciplines and evidence types:
- WHO healthy ageing/ICOPE, ageing and disability research, population longitudinal surveys (e.g. SHARE, DEAS), epidemiology and systematic reviews.
- Qualitative gerontology, co-design, ethnography, interviews, diary and walk-along studies, interviews/conference transcripts, first-person advocacy and participatory action research.
- Care-worker surveys, professional associations, workflows, staffing studies, provider operations and quality inspection reports.
- Hospital and care-provider service catalogues, rehabilitation/care pathways and discharge practices; reverse engineer **recognized jobs**, not unproven outcomes.
- Government/municipal data, reimbursement rules, insurance, legal and social care systems, public infrastructure, economics, cross-national comparison.
- Nonprofit/community, analog and DIY workarounds in addition to digital and commercial responses.

No single population or institutional viewpoint defines all human needs. Record geography, date, methodology, sample/setting and limits; Germany-specific payer claims must not be generalized globally.

## Claim handling

Store specific statements with source locators and what each source actually supports. Distinguish:
- **Documented:** narrowly stated claim grounded in relevant reviewed material.
- **Interpretation:** analyst synthesis inferred from evidence, explicitly marked.
- **Candidate / hypothesis:** something plausible to investigate, not verified.
- **Illustrative:** constructed scenarios, timelines or mock personas for UX testing only.
- **Unknown / contested:** insufficient or competing evidence; preserve counterevidence.

A link is not equivalent to verification. One interview cannot establish prevalence. A company's brochure can substantiate product features, not independent efficacy. A provider catalogue can demonstrate a response, not that the need is adequately met. A historical internal 'kill' conclusion is not proof the human problem disappeared.

## Research protocol

1. Define the question/space without a company list.
2. Conduct top-down and bottom-up sweeps; search for counterexamples and negative evidence.
3. Extract specific affected population, situation, obstacle, observed workaround, mechanisms, outcome and source locator.
4. Deduplicate statements carefully; preserve distinct contexts and stakeholder disagreement.
5. Connect causes/responses to problems with **separate relationship-level evidence**.
6. Mark uncovered geographies, groups, settings, unanswered questions and source coverage.
7. Only then connect existing products/services; document actual capability, buyer/payer **when sourced**, and evaluation limitations.
8. Review a problem space for clarity, representativeness and evidence before editorial storytelling.
9. Keep a change log; do not silently promote a research candidate into a fact.

## Responsible story design

Use real attributed participants only with appropriate permission and source context. Avoid flattening a person's experience into a diagnosis or replacing diverse accounts with a single representative narrative. When prototyping with plausible fictional days, show a prominent **illustrative** label and do not invent measured statistics, outcomes or quotations.

## Data inventory caution

Previous exploratory research reported **48 domains / 384 candidate problems / 50 sources**, but these counts are **not a validated, comprehensive problem map**. The database foundation reports source files not yet recovered there; import only from originals you can inspect and retain source provenance. Don't claim existing 56 company records or legacy T-codes validate coverage.

## Review before publication

For every important claim: can the cited source actually support this scope? Are recency, country, sample and contrary evidence clear? Are causal words stronger than the research permits? Could the UI unintentionally imply opportunity rankings or market size through placement? Can the reader see what we don't know?
