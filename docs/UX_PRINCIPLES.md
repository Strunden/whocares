# Atlas UX: horizontal understanding, vertical depth

> Implementation guidance derived from Who Cares product principles, not an alternative source of truth. See [PRODUCT_VISION.md](PRODUCT_VISION.md) and the database principle version.

## What the venture builder needs

The browse experience is for **venture builders trying to understand and discover**, not for care recipients searching for a service. In a few minutes they should develop a credible mental model of breadth, know whom problems affect and why they matter, and feel motivated to investigate one unfamiliar issue. They decide what is commercially or ethically promising.

## The map *is* the primary interface

**Horizontal moment: understand the whole.** The overview is substantial editorial comprehension, **not just a menu of headings**. Each visible territory needs:
- An intelligible name and short answer to **what's happening here?**
- **Why this matters and to whom**, across relevant stakeholders.
- Its conceptual relationship to adjacent territories, without invented numeric encodings.
- Enough white space that novice eyes can scan the big picture.

The north star **A Life Worth Living** may anchor the composition visually, but is not a literal central stakeholder node or forcing every problem to connect to it.

**Semantic zoom: actually zoom.** Free continuous pan, cursor-centered wheel, pinch and keyboard alternatives; never fake zoom with preset screen increments, and never pick the next topic for a user. At successive scales, reveal additional **problem statements, mechanisms, stakeholders, connections, scene entry points and evidence**. Preserve orientation via stable map geometry or small breadcrumbs/minimap. Do not imply physical map areas represent quantified size.

**Vertical moment: unpack the reality.** On choosing a problem, reveal a compelling **real-world situation**, documentary day-in-life/workflow, constraints and cross-stakeholder consequences; continue into sourced research, workarounds, existing responses, counterevidence and open questions. Stories can unfold within map context. If illustrative, state so prominently; never fake quotes.

## Stakeholder lenses

An illustrated menu may feature older adult, care worker, family carer, provider and other relevant perspectives. Selecting a lens re-emphasizes **the same map** based on their aspirations, jobs, frictions and incentives. The lens should reveal new comprehension, not merely dim nodes. Preserve other perspectives and easy reset.

## Visual direction

- Spacious, editorial, map-first layout with soft translucent **organic territories**, muted differentiated color washes, delicate connections and excellent typography.
- Story-level human illustration, light narrative rhythm and visual evidence in context, inspired by rich editorial data storytelling.
- Avoid default technical graph nodes, boxed dashboards, busy legends, global statistics without context, permanent panels and excessive controls.
- Disclose progressively; hover/focus previews and selection detail should *not* obstruct the map.
- Device/keyboard/screen-reader alternatives and non-motion pathways are first-class.
- Clear affordances distinguish draggable canvas, selectable places and clickable links.

## Keep source integrity visible

Citations on factual claims, distinguish evidence from interpretation; label unsourced sample content. A company's appearance on the map is **evidence of a response**, not proof of problem size or solution success. No venture rankings, attractiveness scores, or moral grading.

## Design-review loop (internal)

Use separate roles for concept creation and critique where independent sessions are available. Freeze critique criteria before seeing prototypes. Evaluate:
1. **Orientation:** Can a newcomer explain the major forces and who matters after ~3 minutes?
2. **Discovery:** Did they encounter a non-obvious problem without needing the right search term?
3. **Mechanism:** Can they explain why it happens and links to other spaces?
4. **Perspective:** Do real bottom-up adaptations and top-down incentives become intelligible?
5. **Evidence:** Can they distinguish sourced facts from conjecture and know what remains unknown?
6. **Agency:** Do they form their own next question instead of following an AI verdict?
7. **Interaction:** Is spatial zoom/pan intuitive, responsive and accessible?
8. **Visual fidelity:** Does it feel like the soft editorial map reference, not a graph dashboard?

Conceptual self-critique is **not** independent user validation. Test with venture builders, compare what they can explain, capture false confidence and measure whether the next research question is specific and relevant. A technically functioning map is insufficient.
