# Browsing directions after the rejected scene trial

Design recommendation, 11 October 2026. Not a completed redesign or evidence of usability gains.

**Latest user correction:** the existing list is already strong. The missing layer is “so what”: significance, weighting and relationships. Preserve the list as the primary browse surface rather than replacing it with the field-guide recommendation below. Treat that recommendation as an explored alternative, not the chosen implementation. The next trial should explain consequence and relationship alongside the existing entries, with direct source scopes and explicit unknowns. No synthetic importance/venture score.

The screenshot illustrates mixed conceptual roles: healthcare access is an enabling condition; rebuilding everyday life is a broad outcome; managing a long-term condition is ongoing work; medicine routines are a concrete task; worry/low mood can intersect several areas. This is an editorial analysis of the labels, not a proven causal model. Review roles and overlaps against source evidence before rewriting identities or drawing causal arrows.

Significance should expose separate evidence dimensions: affected population/reach, frequency or persistence, consequences/effort, and documented limits of existing responses. Each needs geography, population, date, denominator/method where applicable and uncertainty. Unknown means unmeasured, not low importance; sparse catalogue coverage is not an unmet market. Relationship labels distinguish shared context, enables, overlaps and documented consequences; proposed relations remain visibly proposed.

## What the current material supports

The graph has broad knowledge-generated needs and situations, narrower provider-supported offerings and uneven human research. It does not yet support treating every entry as a validated insight, writing representative stories or asserting market gaps. The visitor's task is to learn a space and choose a next investigation. Showing the storage hierarchy directly makes them do our synthesis work.

## Alternatives considered

| Direction | Best contribution | Main limitation for this atlas |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dense searchable catalogue | Fast lookup and comparison of known entries | Weak unfamiliar-space orientation |
| People × responsibilities matrix | Visible breadth and stakeholder differences | Many cells; implies coverage unless gaps are explicit |
| Day-in-life timeline | Context and temporal relationships | Care is not one linear day; current entries lack grounded sequences |
| Turning-point routes | Concrete transitions such as hospital discharge | Omits recurring daily and organisational work |
| Mindmap/network | Cross-connections and exploration | Hundreds of nodes recreate visual overload |
| How-might-we card deck | Immediate ideation prompts | Premature questions can disguise weak evidence and fragment context |
| Documentary scenes | Empathy and causal explanation | Requires sourced accounts; illustrative composites can mislead |
| Existing-response comparison | Mechanisms, access and alternatives | Provider coverage must not define the human problem taxonomy |
| Editorial field guide | Clear areas followed by compact problem briefs | Requires genuine synthesis, explicit boundaries and research status |

## Selected direction: editorial field guide

Use people as a compact perspective selector. Present recognisable parts of that person's life/work with one plain scope sentence and a few concrete examples. Do not start with 50 full first-person statements, a compulsory wizard or three simultaneous lists. Keep direct search available.

Opening an area shows a compact set of concrete difficulties. Selecting one opens a problem brief with its context, related difficulties, supporting/contrary evidence, existing responses and unresolved questions. Keep a small navigable outline for orientation. The original needs, situations and offering identities remain the data model; this is a presentation recommendation, not a graph rewrite.

For family carers, provisional areas could include starting care, everyday caring, arranging reliable support, work and personal life, money and entitlements, changes in care, relationships and choices, and end-of-life/aftercare. Check overlap against actual records; do not declare these exhaustive or duplicate existing entries to fill every area.

A candidate brief such as **Finding the right first contact** can name its intended circumstance without inventing a participant: a family trying to arrange help without knowing where to begin. Related existing hypotheses include unclear first steps, unfamiliar system terms and uncertainty about family/professional responsibilities. These are hypotheses, not established mechanisms. Existing compass/Pflegestützpunkte records supply narrower attributed response facts. A question such as **How might we make the first step toward care clear without requiring families to understand the system first?** remains an editorial investigation prompt.

Scenes become optional explanatory material inside a brief when documented accounts support them. A how-might-we question follows the explanation; it is not a substitute for it. One problem may connect to multiple areas and perspectives without cloning identity.

## Reasons and checks

[NN/g information scent](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/) supports descriptive labels that let visitors anticipate destinations. [Progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) supports separating essential comprehension from detail while warning about excessive depth. [Mapping methods compared](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-mapping-cheat-sheet/) distinguishes journey and service/process explanations rather than treating one map as universally suitable. These support design choices, not measured efficiency for Who Cares.

Compare the existing browser with a realistic field-guide prototype using the same records. Measure orientation, correct problem explanation, successful response finding, specificity of next research questions and false confidence. Preserve breadth, search, source inspection, keyboard access and scoped qualifications. Do not add opportunity scores or imply that missing offerings equal an unmet market.
